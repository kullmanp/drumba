# A Dreierruf is a Figur on its Landung

ADR-0002 puts a **Fünferruf** on its first stroke, with the Landung as the next ordinary
Schlag. A **Dreierruf** is never written that way. It is a small mark of two rolled Dupfe that
stands before its Landung and takes no room in the Takt: its two strokes divide the gap from
the Schlag before them to the Landung into three equal parts. We considered three shapes: the
mark as a property of the Schlag before it, a Figur spanning a whole Tagwachtstreich, and a
Figur on the Landung.

We chose the **Figur on the Landung**, held like the Doublé's soft Dupfe: the Schlag is the
Landung, at its own position, value and Hand, and the two rolled Dupfe in the other Hand belong
to the figure. The mark belongs with the stroke it leads into, not with the stroke it follows.
A Dreierruf without its Landung cannot be written. One Schlag having one Figur costs nothing
here, because a Ruf only ever lands on a Dupfe.

## Consequences

- The two Rufe modelled so far sit on different strokes: a Fünferruf on its first, a
  Dreierruf on its last.
- When the Landung is the first Schlag of a Takt, the Dreierruf belongs to that Takt but is
  drawn before its Taktstrich, at the end of the previous one.
- Its timing reads the Schlag before it, which may be in the previous Takt.
